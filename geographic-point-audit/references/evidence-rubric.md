# Evidence rubric

## Source hierarchy

1. Government or regulator: current official page, announcement, public-service record, or named acceptance list.
2. Venue or operator: official website, verified official account, on-site notice, booking platform.
3. Authoritative media: Xinhua, China News, provincial/municipal party media when quoting named sources.
4. Maps and OTA: location and current status clues only; not sufficient alone for dynamic prices, hours, or booking claims.
5. Blogs, encyclopedias, aggregation pages: discovery only. Do not use them to assert current operation.

Record the publication or update date whenever the page supports one. A 2022 page cannot establish 2026 current operation.

## Current-status evidence

Classify each claim independently:

- **Existence/location**: official address or map plus official-page corroboration.
- **Currently open**: current official statement, dated current announcement, or current operator notice.
- **Free/paid**: current official ticket or fee schedule. A free outdoor park and a paid sub-experience may coexist.
- **Hours/age/bookings**: current operator rule. Event-specific age limits must not be generalized into a permanent rule.
- **Facilities**: official project acceptance, current operator description, or on-site evidence. Project design articles may not prove current equipment operation.

## Observation-only triggers

Keep a point out of the numeric main table when any applies:

- under construction or new venue not yet open;
- suspended or recovery time unknown;
- only historical media proves past operation;
- exact official name/address cannot be established;
- current official source and map/OTA conflict materially;
- the point is a station, route, or scenery sub-area rather than an independent destination.

## Coverage rubric

Search only online-published content unless the user explicitly requests draft coverage.

- **Main story**: the canonical point name or an unambiguous alias appears in frontmatter title or H1 and the body confirms it as the subject.
- **Supporting only**: appears in a title contrast (`除了A，看B`), as a scenic-area subsection, or only in body text.
- **Deleted story**: dedicated prior article exists in the deletion ledger and is no longer online.
- **Zero**: no valid match in published content.

A filename or keyword hit without reading context is not evidence. Watch for:
- abbreviations and superscripts (`E³PARK` vs `E3PARK`);
- poetic or unrelated terms (`白鹭`);
- same names in another district;
- the inventory/report itself;
- drafts and raw research files.

## Deduplication decisions

- Scenic-area pure scenery sub-items: one destination.
- Scenic-area museum with independent opening and booking: may be separately counted, but state the parent scenic area.
- Old and new museum sites during a transition: one point until the old site closes; record current entrance.
- Station themes, bus stops, ferry terminals: transport attributes unless they are themselves a destination.
- A commercial complex and a named indoor attraction inside it: two points only when the attraction has independent current operation, hours, and booking.

## Two official systems: children-friendly parks vs life scenes

Do not merge `儿童友好公园` with `儿童友好生活场景`, `旅游景区`, or administrative labels. Record the exact official wording. If an official article says ten parks passed municipal acceptance but names only five, write both facts: the total and the named subset. Never reconstruct the unnamed five.

## Numeric validation

Generate titles and summaries from the canonical overview table or validate them with a parser. Hand-counted totals drift during edits. Run validation after every change. Use exact Markdown headings to extract sections; substring-based splitting can swallow the summary section and create a false zero-row pass.
